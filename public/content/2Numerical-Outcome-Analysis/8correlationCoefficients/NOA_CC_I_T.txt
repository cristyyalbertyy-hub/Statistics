UNDERSTANDING CORRELATION COEFFICIENTS

This infographic provides a visual guide to interpretation:

THE CONCEPT: Correlation (often denoted by the symbol r or ρ) measures how closely two distinct data sets move in relation to each other, using data collected from independent samples.

KEY GOAL: The goal is to determine if one variable's changes correspond with changes in another, enabling researchers to draw inferences about data relationships.

HOW CORRELATION WORKS
The value always falls on a scale from -1.0 to +1.0.

POSITIVE CORRELATION (+r): As one variable increases, the other also increases. A perfect positive correlation (+1.0) creates a straight, upward line with minimal scatter.

ZERO CORRELATION (0): There is no discernible linear relationship. Data points appear completely scattered with no predictable pattern.

NEGATIVE CORRELATION (-r): As one variable increases, the other decreases. A perfect negative correlation (-1.0) creates a straight, downward line with minimal scatter.

INTERPRETING RESULTS
The decision point often involves comparing the p-value against a significance level (α, such as 0.05) to reject the null hypothesis of no correlation. A smaller Standard Error (SE) indicates greater confidence in the correlation coefficient estimate.
